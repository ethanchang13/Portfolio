What it is

MHSToday is the student-run news site for Manalapan High School. Unlike every other project in this portfolio, it isn't a site I built from scratch — it's a live publication I contribute to. That distinction is the whole point: this is what real digital publishing looks like when there's an audience, an editorial process, and deadlines that don't move.

What I do there

  • Write articles for a real public audience.
  • Contribute photography and visual storytelling.
  • Work inside the existing CMS — formatting, headlines, ledes, media embeds — instead of writing my own markup.
  • Hit deadlines and ship pieces that go straight to readers.

Tech stack

  • SNO Sites (school newsroom CMS)
  • HTML / rich-text content blocks
  • Digital photography
  • Editorial workflow tools

Highlights

Writing for a real audience

Every article goes out to actual readers — students, parents, teachers. That changes how you write a headline and how you cut a paragraph that doesn't earn its space.

Working inside someone else's system

Most of this portfolio is sites I built from scratch. MHSToday is the opposite: an established CMS with established conventions. Learning to ship inside someone else's framework is a different skill than building your own.

Photo + story together

Pieces aren't just text — every story needs imagery that supports it. Sourcing, shooting, cropping, and captioning are part of the job.

What I learned

  • Shipping reliably matters more than reinventing the layout for every piece.
  • Editorial constraints — word counts, headline rules, AP-style conventions — make writing better, not worse.
  • The difference between a personal project (where I control every pixel) and contributing to a live shared product.
  • How a CMS thinks: content blocks, taxonomies, scheduled publishing, and why those abstractions exist.
